# Activity Design Guidelines — ICT

- **Version:** 0.1
- **Date:** 2026-11-24
- **Status:** Teacher-approved working version for template development and pilot testing
- **Audience:** Teaching team and teacher-facing AI assistants
- **Scope:** Theoretical-practical exercises (TP) and practical exercises (P)
- **Location:** `docs/activity-design-guidelines.md`

## 1. Purpose and authority

These guidelines translate the course's pedagogical principles into practical decisions for designing, adapting, reviewing and reusing P and TP activities. Their purpose is to improve instructional coherence, student experience and the efficiency of teacher–AI collaboration without increasing routine administrative work.

Read these guidelines alongside [PEDAGOGY.md](../PEDAGOGY.md) (v1.2 at the time of drafting), especially sections 4–8, 9–11 and 13–18, and the [documentation ecosystem](documentation-ecosystem.md). The official course guide and applicable institutional requirements retain their authority. These guidelines do not introduce new assessment rules, grade weights or mandatory AI workflows.

Consult `content.md`, `ontology.md`, `activities.md`, `assessment.md`, `resources.md` and the edition teaching route when relevant and available. Do not assume a planned module already exists. Where mappings or policies remain unresolved, flag them for teacher review rather than inventing requirements.

## 2. Choose the activity's pedagogical function

Start with the learning need, not with a tool or a predetermined product. Identify what students should understand, do or decide and what evidence would demonstrate that learning.

| Dimension | TP — Theoretical-Practical exercise | P — Practical exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Main function | Apply or consolidate a theoretical concept through a focused task. | Develop applied competence through exploration, analysis, problem-solving, design or creation. |
| Typical setting | Theoretical session; usually brief and individual. | Practical session, with autonomous work where appropriate. P assessment is individual under the current planning model. |
| Evidence | A focused response, explanation, application or other minimum demonstration of learning. | A product, analysis, process record or justified decision appropriate to the intended learning. |
| Assessment | Binary completion: 0 or 100; no direct grade weighting. | Four levels: 0, 33, 66 or 100; contributes to the relevant block grade. |
| Scope | Keep the task manageable within its allotted time. | Adjust complexity and support to prior learning, available time and resources. |

Both types may prepare students for projects or support learning independently. Q questionnaires and Prj project briefs have distinct purposes and are outside the scope of this template.

## 3. Establish constructive alignment

Design backwards from the intended learning:

**Learning purpose → observable objective → student task → evidence → success criteria → feedback or assessment.**

For each activity:

1. Identify the relevant block (A, B or C) and the concepts or content being mobilized.
2. Select the relevant academic competences (AC1–AC5), TPC areas and specific DigCompEdu competences. Distinguish main and secondary DigCompEdu links where useful. Do not confuse the course's TPC areas with individual DigCompEdu competence codes.
3. Link relevant course learning outcomes (LO1–LO6) provisionally until the teaching team validates them. Do not assign additional grade weights to these links.
4. Formulate a small number of activity-specific objectives with observable verbs, such as compare, explain, create, evaluate or justify.
5. Specify the evidence students will produce and how it demonstrates the objectives. Select success criteria that can be applied to that evidence.

Record related activities, concepts and projects when a meaningful relationship exists (e.g., applies a concept, builds a prerequisite or prepares for Prj2). Do not force links merely to populate fields. Avoid inventing content or ontology identifiers; use descriptive references until identifiers are established.

**Illustrative example:** A Block B activity might address TPC2, AC4, DigCompEdu 2.2 and provisional LO4. Its objective is to create and justify an age-appropriate digital resource. Evidence comprises the resource and a short design rationale; criteria address educational suitability, functionality and accessibility. The mappings must be checked against the actual activity, not copied automatically from this example.

## 4. Design the learning experience

### 4.1. Professional relevance and critical judgement

Connect activities to plausible responsibilities of primary school teachers whenever this adds pedagogical value. Explain why a concept, tool or practice matters. Distinguish university students' own digital competence from the digital competence they will help primary school pupils develop.

Technical exploration is a legitimate learning aim. Across the course, however, move beyond operating applications towards choosing, evaluating and adapting technology for educational purposes. Where relevant, ask students to consider limitations, accessibility, risks, and digital or non-digital alternatives. Do not require a lengthy pedagogical justification or reflection in every short exercise.

### 4.2. Scaffolding and autonomy

Select support according to the learning objective, learners' prior experience and task difficulty. The following continuum is a design aid, not a compulsory sequence or student classification:

| Approach | Appropriate support |
|---|---|
| Guided exploration | Worked examples, explicit steps and troubleshooting guidance. |
| Guided application | Partial instructions, examples and checkpoints; students make some decisions. |
| Problem-solving | A clear challenge, constraints and criteria; students choose strategies. |
| Open design and reflection | A purpose, audience and expected evidence; students justify decisions and evaluate alternatives. |

Allow students to make decisions whenever those decisions are the intended learning. Avoid step-by-step instructions that perform the reasoning for them. Provide optional technical help or extension challenges when useful; extensions must not become undisclosed grading requirements.

### 4.3. Evidence, feasibility and inclusion

Distinguish the **outcome** (what students produce), **learning evidence** (what demonstrates their learning) and **submission requirements** (what and how they submit). Request only evidence that serves a defined learning or assessment purpose. A screenshot or completed product alone may not demonstrate reasoning; add a brief explanation, test or process record when necessary.

Estimate workload realistically, including account setup, technical problems and autonomous work. Check resource availability, licences, privacy, accessibility and language demands. Provide feasible alternatives that preserve the learning objective when access barriers arise. Use synthetic or appropriately anonymized data where sufficient. Do not require paid services or unnecessary personal data without justification and suitable alternatives.

## 5. Design assessment and feedback together

### 5.1. Success criteria

Select only the relevant quality dimensions from PEDAGOGY.md §11: conceptual accuracy, pedagogical judgement, instructional coherence, technical quality, evidence and sources, accessibility and inclusion, ethics and responsibility, reflection and autonomy, communication, and authorship and collaboration.

Translate them into observable, activity-specific success criteria (normally two to five, fewer for a simple TP where sufficient). Use precise language. For example, replace “good presentation” with “the resource uses readable text and accurate content appropriate to the selected primary education level”. Avoid grading visual sophistication unless it serves an explicit learning aim.

Make all assessment criteria and minimum completion requirements visible to students before they undertake the activity. Internal notes may clarify how to locate and interpret evidence, but must not introduce additional criteria, hidden penalties or higher expectations.

### 5.2. P and TP scoring

Use the scales in PEDAGOGY.md §9.4. For P, design criteria that distinguish partial evidence, adequate achievement and full achievement of the expected standard (33, 66 and 100), as well as absence of assessable evidence (0). A score of 100 means meeting the published standard, not producing an exceptionally elaborate or flawless product beyond the task requirements.

For TP, explicitly identify the minimum evidence needed for completion (100); non-completion is recorded as 0. Completion is not equivalent to submission alone when the required evidence is absent.

The numerical scales are selected for 2026–2027. The operational descriptors, late-work arrangements, aggregation and recovery procedures remain subject to review. Do not invent criterion weights, averaging formulas, automatic penalties or progression thresholds. Apply formal assessment conditions only after the necessary institutional alignment and teacher confirmation.

### 5.3. Feedback and self-assessment

Write criteria that students can also use to check their work before submitting it. Where appropriate, include a checkpoint, revision opportunity, peer review or focused reflection. Do not impose these in every activity.

When detailed feedback is useful, use the structure: observed evidence → achievement → priority for improvement → concrete next step. Routine exercises do not require individualized written feedback by default. Do not promise feedback or revision opportunities that the teaching team has not arranged.

## 6. Design AI use around the learning objective

### 6.1. Activity-level conditions

Specify the applicable AI-use condition in the student-facing overview and clarify phase-specific rules when necessary:

| Condition | Design implication |
|---|---|
| Permitted | Identify compatible forms of assistance and preserve student responsibility. |
| Restricted | Specify allowed purposes or phases and what students must perform independently. |
| Required | Identify the AI-mediated action that is part of the intended learning, its evidence and access arrangements. |
| Prohibited | Specify the task or phase that must be performed without AI. |

Distinguish authorization from acknowledgement and critical reflection. Where a declaration is required, request proportionate information: tool, purpose, process or sections affected, contribution and limitations, verification and the student's decisions. Request supporting excerpts or screenshots only when justified; do not require complete conversation histories by default. AI acknowledgement does not replace source citation.

### 6.2. Future AI-assisted formative review and assessment support

The internal specification should make the intended learning, evidence and public criteria sufficiently explicit to support future supervised AI workflows without requiring a separate agent specification for each activity now.

Two possible uses are:

- **Student-facing formative support:** review a draft, identify possible errors or inconsistencies, ask guiding questions and help students decide how to revise. Preserve independent performance and avoid supplying the assessed solution.
- **Teacher-facing assessment support:** identify evidence relevant to each published criterion, distinguish observations from inferences, identify missing or uninspectable evidence, and propose feedback or a non-binding assessment recommendation for teacher review.

If a future pilot requires students to interact with an assistant, specify when and why, the permitted assistance, what evidence of the interaction is needed, and an accessible equivalent route where needed. Student revision and judgement should matter more than the volume of AI interaction. Do not treat an assistant's approval as evidence of learning or a submission requirement unless separately justified and approved.

For any assessment-support pilot, test the assistant against teacher-reviewed examples, including borderline cases and incomplete or inaccessible evidence. Review consistency, accuracy, fairness, usefulness, privacy and workload before wider deployment. Missing or uninspectable evidence must be flagged rather than automatically treated as failure. The teacher retains authority over grades, completion and progression decisions. Neither mandatory assistant use nor automated grading is adopted by these guidelines.

## 7. Document and publish the activity

Maintain two complementary layers, following the documentation ecosystem:

| Internal specification | Student-facing brief |
|---|---|
| Stable `ACT-xxx` identifier and revision; P/TP type and block. | Edition-specific code (e.g., `P_S5`), title and textual block label. |
| Pedagogical rationale, content and AC/TPC/DigCompEdu mappings; provisional LO links. | Learning objectives, visible DigCompEdu and academic competence links. |
| Optional relationships, scaffolding rationale, teacher notes and evidence-review guidance. | Time, working mode, resources, expected outcome, context when useful and instructions. |
| Evidence, public criteria and applicable assessment reference; AI conditions. | The same evidence and public criteria, completion/assessment information, submission requirements and AI conditions; reflection or declaration when relevant. |

Use the forthcoming `activities/templates/activity-template.md` as the common semantic structure and `activities/templates/activity-template.docx` as the student-facing visual format. Use ordinary Word headings, simple tables, lists, descriptive links and accessible contrast. Maintain yellow activity headings and a textual block label with A green, B blue or C orange, subject to the planned portability test. Colour and icons must not be the only carriers of meaning.

The Markdown specification is the shared reference for student-facing content, not a second independent set of instructions. Teachers may draft in Word or Markdown; reconcile substantive edits into the specification before publication. Transfer only the student-facing layer to eGela, check rendering and content, and ensure students receive the current requirements. Keep internal notes out of the published brief. eGela remains the operational environment for students.

## 8. Reuse, revise and maintain

When adapting a historical activity, review its learning purpose, resources, AI conditions, assessment scale and curricular mappings. Historical PS identifiers and TPC mappings must not be reused automatically.

Assign the permanent activity ID when registering the activity in `activities.md`. Keep stable content separate from the edition-specific weekly code, deadline and implementation arrangements in the teaching route. Include the effective arrangements in the published brief.

Retain the ID for revisions of the same pedagogical activity; allocate a new ID for a genuinely different reusable activity. As a proposed lightweight convention, record a revision when a change affects learning objectives, evidence, criteria, instructions, tools or AI conditions materially; routine scheduling changes belong in the edition route. Do not silently change published assessment conditions. Record and communicate necessary changes consistently.

## 9. Teacher–AI design and review workflow

Use the following workflow for creating or revising activities; apply it proportionately, not as a separate form to complete for every task.

1. **Consult:** Read the relevant course documents and the existing activity, if any. Identify confirmed decisions and unresolved issues.
2. **Design:** Select the learning purpose, activity type, objectives, evidence, criteria and appropriate level of scaffolding. Check workload, resources and AI-use conditions.
3. **Specify:** Draft or update the internal Markdown specification and the student-facing layer. Flag assumptions, provisional mappings and material changes.
4. **Review:** Use the checklist below; the teacher confirms the design and resolves issues that affect assessment, access, privacy or learning.
5. **Publish and improve:** Prepare the Word brief, check eGela, and use observed difficulties or feedback to improve the activity. Update the register and edition route when available.

AI may propose content, mappings, criteria, scaffolding and feedback approaches, but must not invent course rules, claim verification it has not performed, allocate unconfirmed identifiers, or silently modify approved material. The teacher retains approval. Do not publish identifiable student records or AI interaction histories in GitHub, even if the repository becomes private.

### Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] The activity has a clear purpose and appropriate P/TP scope; the student task is understandable in accessible English.
- [ ] Objectives, curricular links, evidence and success criteria are coherent; provisional mappings are identified internally.
- [ ] The activity supports professional learning and uses technology only when justified by its purpose; the degree of guidance is appropriate.
- [ ] Students can identify what to produce, what evidence is required, how it will be assessed or marked complete, and how and when to submit.
- [ ] Criteria and AI-use conditions are explicit; no undisclosed requirements or unresolved assessment rules are presented as approved.
- [ ] Workload, resource access, privacy and accessibility have been considered; resources and links have been checked where relevant.
- [ ] The student-facing brief matches the internal specification, contains no internal-only notes and remains understandable without visual styling.
- [ ] The teacher has approved publication; the current edition code and arrangements are correct, and the eGela rendering is checked when publishing.

This checklist is a review aid, not additional student assessment criteria or a mandatory stored record.

## 10. Pilot and review

Following teacher validation on 2026-11-24, use these guidelines to develop the Word template first and the Markdown template second. Pilot both with a short TP, a technical P and a more complex AI-related P. Check the time required to design and edit each, clarity of the brief, interpretation by AI, assessment usability and actual Word-to-eGela transfer. Refine the guidelines and templates based on the pilot before consolidating them.

Open matters remain governed by PEDAGOGY.md §18: validation of LO mappings, institutional assessment alignment, operational P/TP descriptors and late-work/recovery arrangements, annual planning, and any future AI pilots. No activity-specific mandatory AI workflow, extra grading dimension or new policy is introduced here.
